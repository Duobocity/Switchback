      How the fun score works

      Each route is resampled every 20 m and the radius of every bend is measured, the same method the Curvature project (roadcurvature.com) uses to rank roads. Corners near turns and intersections don't count, so a city grid doesn't look twisty.

      • Curves, 40%. Share of the drive spent in corners tighter than 175 m radius. Tighter corners are weighted up to 2×. Full marks at 35%.
      • Open road, 20%. Share of the drive off interstates and out of town.
      • Hills, 15%. Feet climbed per mile from elevation data. Full marks at 80 ft/mi.
      • Traffic, 15%. Live delay as a share of drive time. Zero points at 25% delay.
      • Flow, 10%. Fewer turns and junctions per 10 miles means fewer stops.

      “Best stretches” are runs of corners broken up by straights longer than 1.5 miles, scored on Curvature's scale: 300+ has a few good corners, 1,000+ is a fun road, 3,000+ is worth a trip.
