        How the score and the optimizer work

        The route is traced every 10 m and the bend radius is measured from how much the road's heading turns over 60 m. Tight corners count more, following the Curvature project (roadcurvature.com). Bends at junctions, on interstates and on ramps don't count, and bends in town count half.

        • Curves. Weighted corner length per mile. Sweepers (under 400 m radius) count a little, corners under 175 m count fully, hairpins count double. Full marks at 350 per mile, a drive that is mostly twisty back roads.
        • Hills. Feet climbed per mile. Full marks at 70 ft/mi.
        • Open road. Share of the drive off interstates and out of town.
        • Traffic and Stops and turns take points away instead of adding them. At 10, heavy traffic or constant turns can cut the score in half. At 0 they're ignored.

        What's a good score? Under 20 is a city drive. 20–40 has some good stretches. 40–60 is a good drive. 60–75 is great, mostly twisty back roads. 75+ is the best there is around here. Loops starting downtown spend a lot of time getting out of town, so 40–50 is a strong result for a 90-minute loop from downtown. Longer loops, or starting farther out, can reach 70+.

        Loops. Maximize takes the fastest roads out to a “fun zone”, drives a twisty loop there, and takes the fastest roads back. It tests 24 zones (8 directions at 3 distances), fine-tunes the best 4 to fit your time, then tries nudging the winner. A to B does the same with detours off either side of the direct route.

        Road ratings use Curvature's scale: 300+ has a few good corners, 1,000+ is a fun road, 3,000+ is worth a trip.
